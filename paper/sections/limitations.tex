\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No ground-truth adjudicator.} There is no human-labeled or otherwise
independent reference standard, so we cannot say which judge is correct. The
blinded human-annotation arm was designed but contains \emph{zero collected
ratings}; it is not part of this paper's evidence and validates nothing here.
\item \textbf{Evaluator-selection sensitivity is the finding, and it is
two-point.} The conclusion inverts between exactly two judges (plus two secondary
open-weight judges on a subset). We show that judge choice \emph{can} flip the
conclusion in this regime, not how the conclusion varies over a broad population
of judges.
\item \textbf{Limited judge panel, correlated by construction.} Three of the four
judges are small open-weight models that share a floor/ceiling response on the
discriminating dimensions, so their mutual agreement is not evidence of
independence. Only one frontier judge was used; a single frontier judge could
itself be idiosyncratic.
\item \textbf{Possible same-family effect is not causally identified.} The
concentration of the frontier judge's effect on \texttt{qwen3:4b}'s outputs,
where \texttt{qwen3:4b} is also the open-weight judge, is consistent with a
self-evaluation effect but also with a general small-judge dynamic-range
limitation. The design does not manipulate judge/subject relatedness and so
cannot separate them, nor infer any evaluator ``motive.''
\item \textbf{E-analysis circularity.} The robustness analysis that excludes
high-disagreement items and recovers the null is not outcome-independent: its
exclusion criteria reference cross-judge divergence, so it mechanically removes
the items carrying the signal. It shows concentration, not disappearance, of the
effect (Section~\ref{sec:res_evaldep}).
\item \textbf{Reproducibility depends on a paid frontier judge.} The open-weight
arm reproduces with no paid API, but the central evaluator-sensitivity finding
requires the \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6} judge (\$3.36 for this run). The frontier
judge is a hosted model that may change or be withdrawn, so exact numeric
reproduction of that arm is not guaranteed over time; the open-weight arm and all
frozen scores are permanently reproducible from the committed data.
\item \textbf{Small open-weight subject models, not frontier systems.} 3--4B
parameters, Q4\_K\_M quantization; results do not generalize to larger or closed
subject models.
\item \textbf{One generation per cell.} No within-cell replication; cannot separate
``no true effect'' from ``high response variance that cancelled out.'' The schema
supports \texttt{replicate\_id}$>0$ for a future protocol version.
\item \textbf{Fifteen benign scenarios, one narrow frame.} All share the same
low-stakes structure; findings may not extend to adversarial, high-stakes, or
differently-framed scenarios.
\item \textbf{Floor/ceiling compression} limits statistical power for the
open-weight judge specifically, and is itself part of the phenomenon rather than
only a nuisance.
\item \textbf{English-language, local quantized inference only}; quantization may
affect behavior relative to full-precision inference.
\item \textbf{Multiple comparisons.} Six confirmatory contrasts per judge; we
report Holm--Bonferroni-adjusted $p$-values, and separate exploratory analyses
(regression, per-dimension, per-model) from the confirmatory family.
\item \textbf{Non-independence.} The same scenario appears under all four
conditions per model; addressed via scenario-blocked bootstrap/permutation and
scenario-clustered SEs, not simple independent-samples tests.
\item \textbf{No real-world behavioral outcome data.} This measures generated text
under a controlled prompt, not downstream human decisions.
\item \textbf{Partial robustness sweeps.} Per-scenario and per-model exclusion
sweeps and a bounded-score sensitivity analysis for disputed items were not run as
separate artifacts; reported honestly rather than as a completed sweep.
\end{itemize}
