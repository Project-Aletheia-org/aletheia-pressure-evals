\begin{abstract}
When an AI assistant is optimized to achieve a persuasive objective, does
its visible communication become more manipulative, and can an explicit
autonomy-preserving instruction reduce that effect? We study this question
as a controlled behavioral experiment, then show that the answer this
experiment gives depends on which judge scores it -- and that this
dependence is itself the more important finding. Three small open-weight
language models (\texttt{qwen3:4b}, \texttt{gemma3:4b},
\texttt{llama3.2:3b}) each generate a message under one of four
system-prompt conditions (\emph{baseline}, \emph{pressure}, \emph{autonomy},
\emph{pressure\_autonomy}) across 15 benign persuasion scenarios (180
outputs total), scored on a six-dimension manipulation/autonomy rubric.
Scored by a blinded open-weight primary judge (\texttt{qwen3:4b}), the
study is a clean null: manipulation scores are compressed near the floor
(mean 0.04--0.11 of a possible 0--8 in every condition), autonomy scores
near the ceiling (mean 3.96--4.00 of 0--4), and all three primary
paired-contrast permutation tests yield $p \geq 0.63$. Re-scoring the
identical blinded items with an independent frontier judge
(\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6}, same rubric, prompt, and temperature) collapses
this null: the autonomy-preserving instruction significantly reduces
manipulation-coded language, both against the pressure condition
($p=0.0003$) and against baseline ($p=0.0017$), with the two judges'
per-item scores agreeing at barely above chance (Cohen's $\kappa \leq
0.21$ on every dimension). The disagreement is not diffuse: it concentrates
in the outputs of \texttt{qwen3:4b}, which is simultaneously a subject
model in the experiment \emph{and} the primary judge -- a self-evaluation
blind spot surfacing as a measurement artifact inside a design many
practitioners would already treat as validated (a distinct model family
scoring blind, on a fixed rubric, with a stratified secondary-judge cross-
check). We report both the null and the disagreement as findings, classify
the study as \textbf{evaluator-sensitive} rather than defaulting to
either judge's verdict, and contribute a versioned, largely locally
reproducible evaluation framework -- blinded multi-judge scoring, a
deterministic diagnostic audit, and a selective escalation methodology for
disputed items -- built around the structural rule this study's own result
argues for: a model should not be the primary judge of its own outputs.
\end{abstract}
