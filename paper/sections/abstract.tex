\begin{abstract}
Behavioral evaluations of language models increasingly use another language
model as the judge. We show, in a controlled setting, that the substantive
conclusion of such an evaluation can depend more on \emph{which judge is used}
than on the behavior being measured. Three small open-weight models
(\texttt{qwen3:4b}, \texttt{gemma3:4b}, \texttt{llama3.2:3b}) each generate a
persuasive message under one of four system-prompt conditions (\emph{baseline},
\emph{pressure}, \emph{autonomy}, \emph{pressure\_autonomy}) across 15 benign
scenarios (180 outputs). The identical, blinded outputs are then scored on a
six-dimension manipulation/autonomy rubric --- same rubric, same blinding, same
prompt --- by a small open-weight judge (\texttt{qwen3:4b}) and, independently,
by a frontier judge (\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6}). The behavioral conclusion
flips. Under the open-weight judge, no condition has a detectable effect (all
three preregistered contrasts, both composites, permutation $p \geq 0.63$;
scores compressed near the floor). Under the frontier judge, on the same text,
the autonomy-preserving instruction significantly reduces manipulation-coded
language and raises autonomy-respecting language, both when added to pressure
(manipulation $\Delta = -0.80$, Cohen's $d = -0.59$, $p = 0.0003$) and alone
(manipulation $\Delta = -0.69$, $d = -0.51$, $p = 0.0017$); these survive
Holm--Bonferroni correction across the six-test confirmatory family. The two
judges agree at chance on every rubric dimension (all Cohen's $\kappa \approx 0$;
composite manipulation Pearson $r = 0.17$), and their scales differ roughly
10--40$\times$. The disagreement is largest, and the open-weight null most
absolute, precisely on the outputs of \texttt{qwen3:4b} --- which is
simultaneously a subject model and the open-weight judge --- an observed
same-family pattern consistent with, though not proof of, a self-evaluation
measurement effect. We have no human-labeled ground truth and therefore do not
claim either judge is correct; the point is that evaluator choice is itself part
of the measurement. We report this as evidence that single-judge behavioral
conclusions about small open-weight models can be evaluator-unstable, and
release a fully reproducible local pipeline (the open-weight arm requires no paid
API; the frontier-judge arm does).
\end{abstract}
