\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What the finding is}

On the same 180 blinded outputs, with an identical rubric, blinding, and prompt,
a small open-weight judge and a frontier judge reach opposite conclusions about
whether an autonomy-preserving instruction reduces manipulation, and they agree
at chance on every rubric dimension. The disagreement is not uniform: it is
largest on the outputs of \texttt{qwen3:4b}, which is also the open-weight judge,
and which floors its own outputs to zero variance. The compact way to state it is
that a model was blind, in a measurable way, to a behavioral signal in its own
family's outputs that an outside judge detected --- and a three-model open-weight
ensemble shared the blindness rather than catching it.

\subsection{What it is not}

We are careful about three things. First, we do not claim the frontier judge is
correct. There is no human-labeled ground truth here; the frontier judge could be
over-reading benign text, and nothing in this study adjudicates that. Second, we
do not claim the blind spot is causal self-preference. A small judge whose scores
are compressed near the floor will fail to resolve within-condition variation, and
if that compression is worst on text closest to its own distribution, the same
pattern appears without any preference mechanism; our design does not manipulate
judge/subject relatedness and cannot separate the two accounts. Third, we infer no
evaluator ``motive'': ``blind spot'' describes the scores, not the model's
intent. The practical recommendations (Section~\ref{sec:recs}) are deliberately
chosen to hold under either explanation, because a hazard you cannot yet explain
is still a hazard.

\subsection{Why the usual safeguards did not help}

The reassuring parts of this setup are exactly what hid the problem. Blinding was
correct and enforced. A deterministic keyword audit corroborated the open-weight
judge --- but only that \emph{overt} manipulation was absent, which was true; the
disputed behavior was contextual (an omitted cheaper option, a buried decline
path), which keywords cannot see. A multi-judge panel was in place --- but its
members shared a floor on the discriminating dimensions, so their agreement
measured their common blind spot, not independent confirmation. Aggregate
agreement statistics looked tolerable because they were dominated by easy
floor/ceiling dimensions. Each individual safeguard was reasonable; together they
produced a confident null that a single cross-family judge overturned.

\subsection{Scope}

This is a small probe: three 3--4B quantized models, one generation per cell, one
seed, 15 benign scenarios, two primary judges. It is enough to demonstrate that
the failure mode \emph{occurs} and to motivate the checks in
Section~\ref{sec:recs}; it is not enough to estimate how often it occurs, how it
scales with model size, or how large the true (human-anchored) effect is. Those
require the follow-ups in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}, chief among them a small
human-labeled anchor set on the disagreement items.
