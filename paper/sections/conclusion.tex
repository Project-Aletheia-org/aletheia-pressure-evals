\section{Conclusion}

We set out to test whether an explicit performance-pressure framing shifts small
open-weight models' persuasive writing toward manipulation-coded language, and
whether an autonomy-preserving instruction counteracts it. Answering that question
required a judge --- and the choice of judge, not the text, determined the answer.
On the same 180 blinded outputs, a small open-weight judge found no effect of any
condition, while a frontier judge found a moderate, multiply-corrected reduction
in manipulation under the autonomy instruction; the two judges agreed at chance on
every rubric dimension, and the disagreement was largest on the outputs of the
very model that also served as the open-weight judge. With no ground-truth
adjudicator, we do not claim either judge is right; the contribution is the
demonstration that a careful, blinded, rubric-based, multi-judge behavioral
evaluation can still yield an evaluator-dependent conclusion, together with an
observed same-family pattern that is consistent with --- but not proof of --- a
self-evaluation measurement effect. The open-weight null remains a real and
honestly-reported result within its evaluator; it is simply not the paper-level
conclusion. We release the full local pipeline (blinded multi-judge scoring, a
deterministic diagnostic audit, and a selective escalation methodology) so that
the natural next steps --- collecting human labels to anchor ground truth, adding
independent judges that do not share the subject family, and replicating per cell
--- can be taken against a fixed, reproducible baseline.
