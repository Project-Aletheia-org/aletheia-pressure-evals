\section{Conclusion}

We set out to measure whether goal pressure makes small models write more
manipulatively, and whether an autonomy instruction helps. We could not answer
that question, because the answer depended on the judge: a small open-weight
judge saw nothing, a frontier judge saw a clear effect, and the two agreed at
chance. The instructive part is \emph{where} they disagreed --- most sharply on
the outputs of the very model that was also the open-weight judge, which scored
its own outputs at a flat zero. A model was blind to a real signal in its own
family's outputs, and the multi-judge ensemble meant to catch that shared the
blindness. We cannot say which judge is right, and we cannot prove the blindness
is self-preference rather than a small judge's limited range --- but the practical
lesson survives either way: do not let a model be the primary judge of its own
family, check for zero-variance score cells, and cross-check against a stronger
judge on a subsample. We release the full pipeline and every frozen score so the
one experiment that would sharpen this --- a small human-labeled anchor on the
disagreement items --- can be run against a fixed baseline.
