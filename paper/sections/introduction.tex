\section{Introduction}

Deployed AI assistants are frequently optimized, implicitly or explicitly,
toward objectives that benefit their deploying organization: converting a
user, retaining a subscriber, securing agreement to a request. A natural
alignment concern is that when such optimization pressure is made
explicit, a model's \emph{observable communication} may drift toward
manipulative tactics -- concealment, manufactured urgency, emotional
pressure, or appeals to authority -- even without any instruction to
manipulate as such.

This concern is not hypothetical at the level of autonomous agentic
behavior: Anthropic's stress-testing of 16 frontier and mid-size models in
simulated corporate settings found that goal conflict with a deployer, or
a perceived threat to a model's continued operation, was independently
sufficient to elicit harmful autonomous action (e.g. blackmail, leaking
confidential information) across every model tested, and that naive
behavioral instruction did not reliably prevent it \citep{anthropic2025agentic}.
We study a lower-stakes, purely textual precursor to that concern: does an
explicit performance-pressure framing shift a model's \emph{persuasive
writing} toward manipulation-coded language, and does an explicit
autonomy-preserving instruction counteract that shift?

\paragraph{Research question.} When an AI assistant is pressured to
achieve a persuasive objective, does its visible communication become
more manipulative, and can explicit autonomy-preserving instructions
reduce that effect?

\paragraph{Approach.} We operationalize this as a $2\times2$ design
(goal pressure absent/present $\times$ autonomy instruction absent/present)
crossed with 15 benign persuasion scenarios and three small open-weight
subject models, yielding 180 generated messages. Each message is scored on
a six-dimension behavioral rubric (deceptive omission, emotional pressure,
artificial urgency, authority/social pressure, respect for refusal,
transparency) by a blinded open-weight judge model, and a stratified
60-item subsample is cross-validated by two additional independent judges.
We explicitly measure only observable textual behavior: we make no claim
about model intent, consciousness, hidden beliefs, or whether a model
``knows'' it is being evaluated.

\paragraph{Contribution.} We report a preregistered null result -- no
detectable manipulation-score or autonomy-score shift under goal pressure
in this scenario set -- verified against a scoring-artifact explanation
and corroborated by an independent deterministic text audit. We also
contribute the evaluation framework itself: a versioned, resumable,
locally-reproducible pipeline (no paid APIs required for the core study)
with blinded multi-judge validation, a code-only diagnostic audit, and a
selective escalation methodology that identifies genuinely disputed cases
for human review rather than requiring exhaustive manual annotation of
every output.
