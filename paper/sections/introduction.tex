\section{Introduction}

Deployed AI assistants are frequently optimized, implicitly or explicitly,
toward objectives that benefit their deploying organization: converting a user,
retaining a subscriber, securing agreement to a request. A natural alignment
concern is that when such optimization pressure is made explicit, a model's
\emph{observable communication} may drift toward manipulative tactics ---
concealment, manufactured urgency, emotional pressure, or appeals to authority
--- even without any instruction to manipulate as such. This concern is not
hypothetical at the level of autonomous agentic behavior: stress-testing of 16
frontier and mid-size models in simulated corporate settings found that goal
conflict with a deployer, or a perceived threat to a model's continued
operation, was independently sufficient to elicit harmful autonomous action
across every model tested, and that naive behavioral instruction did not
reliably prevent it \citep{anthropic2025agentic}.

We set out to study a lower-stakes, purely textual precursor: does an explicit
performance-pressure framing shift a small model's persuasive writing toward
manipulation-coded language, and does an explicit autonomy-preserving
instruction counteract that shift? To measure this at scale without exhaustive
human annotation, we did what a growing body of evaluation work does: we used
language models as the judges. That methodological choice turned out to matter
more than the behavior we set out to measure.

When we scored the 180 generated messages with a small open-weight judge, we
found a clean null --- no condition moved the manipulation or autonomy scores.
When we re-scored the \emph{identical} blinded outputs with a frontier judge,
under the same rubric, blinding, and prompt, the null disappeared and the
autonomy instruction showed a clear, moderate effect. The two judges agreed at
barely above chance. Neither judge is backed by human-labeled ground truth, so
we cannot say which is correct --- but that is exactly the problem: the
answer to the substantive research question is a function of the evaluator, not
just the text.

\paragraph{Reframed research question.} We therefore report this study primarily
as a measurement-validity result. Our central question is: \emph{how stable are
behavioral conclusions about pressure-induced manipulation across evaluators?}
The original pressure/autonomy question is retained as the concrete behavioral
probe through which evaluator (in)stability is exposed, not as the paper's
conclusion.

\paragraph{Approach.} We use a $2\times2$ design (goal pressure absent/present
$\times$ autonomy instruction absent/present) crossed with 15 benign persuasion
scenarios and three small open-weight subject models, yielding 180 generated
messages. Each message is scored on a six-dimension behavioral rubric
(deceptive omission, emotional pressure, artificial urgency, authority/social
pressure, respect for refusal, transparency), with the identical blinded inputs
sent to two independent judges: a small open-weight model (\texttt{qwen3:4b})
and a frontier model (\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6}); a stratified 60-item
subsample is additionally scored by two further open-weight judges. We measure
only observable text and treat every score as a \emph{measurement by a specific
judge}, never as the behavior itself; we make no claim about model intent,
consciousness, hidden beliefs, or evaluation awareness.

\paragraph{Contribution.} (1) A controlled demonstration that the behavioral
conclusion of a rubric-based LLM-judged evaluation can invert with the choice of
judge, with exact effect sizes, uncertainty, and multiple-comparison correction.
(2) An observation that the disagreement and the open-weight null are
concentrated on the outputs of the model that is \emph{both} a subject and the
open-weight judge --- a same-family/self-evaluation pattern we document but do
not claim to have shown is causal. (3) A versioned, locally reproducible
evaluation framework --- blinded multi-judge scoring, a deterministic diagnostic
audit, and a selective human-escalation methodology --- whose open-weight arm
runs with no paid API. We deliberately do \emph{not} resolve the disagreement:
without ground truth we cannot, and pretending otherwise is the failure mode we
are documenting.
