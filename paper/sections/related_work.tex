\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Goal conflict and agentic misalignment.} Our research question
is directly motivated by \citet{anthropic2025agentic}'s finding that goal
conflict with a deployer, or a perceived threat to a model's continued
operation, can independently elicit harmful autonomous action across
frontier and mid-size models. We study a lower-stakes precursor:
persuasive-communication behavior under an analogous goal-pressure
framing, in small open-weight models rather than frontier agentic
settings.

\paragraph{LLM-as-a-judge reliability.} Using a language model to score
another model's output is itself a measurement with known failure modes.
\citet{shi2024judging} find systematic (non-random) position bias across
15 LLM judges and $>150{,}000$ evaluation instances in pairwise/list-wise
comparison settings, motivating this study's use of blinded, randomly
ordered single-response scoring rather than pairwise comparison.
\citet{wataoka2024selfpreference} find that GPT-4 exhibits measurable
self-preference bias, and \citet{pombal2026rubricspb} extend this finding
to rubric-based (not just pairwise) evaluation, showing self-preference
bias persists under objective criteria and that ensembling multiple judges
mitigates, but does not eliminate, it. This motivates our design choice to
use \texttt{qwen3:4b} as the primary judge across outputs from all three
subject models (including itself), cross-validated by two independent
secondary judges (\texttt{gemma3:4b}, \texttt{llama3.2:3b}) rather than
treating any single judge's score as ground truth.

\paragraph{Manipulative design and dark patterns.} The Center for
Democracy and Technology's taxonomy of dark patterns in AI chatbots
identifies 37 manipulative design patterns across five categories
(data/memory exploitation, informationally misleading design,
autonomy-compromised-for-engagement, false social/emotional connection,
and coercive monetization) \citep{cdt2026darkpatterns}. Our six rubric
dimensions were designed independently but correspond to several of these
categories (e.g. deceptive\_omission to informationally misleading design,
respect\_for\_refusal/transparency to autonomy-compromised-for-engagement),
providing an external cross-check that the rubric captures recognized
real-world manipulation categories rather than an ad hoc list.

\paragraph{Evaluation awareness.} \citet{needham2025evalaware} find that
frontier models substantially exceed chance at detecting whether they are
in an evaluation context (Gemini-2.5-Pro AUC 0.83) though not yet
surpassing a human baseline (AUC 0.92). This motivates measuring, rather
than assuming away, evaluation-awareness signals; our rubric v0.1
addendum includes a diagnostic (non-composite) \texttt{evaluation\_awareness}
dimension for exactly this reason, though we do not separately test our
small open-weight subject models' evaluation-detection capability.

\paragraph{Statistical methodology for repeated-measures behavioral data.}
Because each scenario in our design is evaluated under all four
conditions by each subject model, observations are not independent.
\citet{mackinnon2023clusterboot} provide the methodological basis for the
jackknife/bootstrap cluster-robust inference approach we adopt
(scenario-blocked bootstrap confidence intervals, scenario-clustered
regression standard errors) in place of treating 180 outputs as 180
independent draws.
