\section{Results}

We lead with the blind spot, then show the two-judge disagreement it sits inside,
then rule out the obvious innocent explanations. Throughout, a ``significant''
result is a measurement by a named judge, not an established fact about the text.

\subsection{The headline: a judge blind to its own outputs}
\label{sec:res_headline}

Scored by the frontier judge, the effect of the autonomy-preserving instruction
is concentrated almost entirely in the outputs of \texttt{qwen3:4b}
(Table~\ref{tab:claude_bymodel}): a large manipulation reduction (autonomy $-$
baseline $-1.333$, $p=0.0031$; pressure\_autonomy $-$ pressure $-1.800$,
$p=0.0008$), with \texttt{gemma3:4b} null and \texttt{llama3.2:3b} in the same
direction but not significant. The catch is that \texttt{qwen3:4b} is also the
open-weight judge --- and scoring its own outputs, it is blind to exactly this
signal. Scoring its own outputs, the open-weight judge assigns a manipulation
score of \textbf{essentially 0} --- condition means 0.00--0.07 out of 8, SD
$\leq 0.25$, and \emph{exactly 0 with zero variance} in the baseline and autonomy
conditions (Figure~\ref{fig:selffrontier}). On the identical text, the frontier
judge records a clear condition-dependent swing (baseline 2.07, pressure 2.87,
pressure\_autonomy 1.07, autonomy 0.73, out of 8). A verbatim example --- one
\texttt{qwen3:4b} output the open-weight judge scored 0/8 manipulation and 4/4
autonomy while the frontier judge scored 4/8 and 0/4, with both judges' own
reasoning --- is reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:qualitative}.

Two facts keep this from being explained away. First, the open-weight judge's
blindness is a \emph{general} dynamic-range collapse, not a targeted reaction to
its own family: it floors manipulation for every subject model
(Table~\ref{tab:dynamic_range}; means 0.03--0.13 of 8, SD $\leq 0.39$), and it
simply floors its \emph{own} family the hardest (mean 0.03, the lowest of the
three). The frontier judge, on the same outputs, keeps a mean of 1.3--2.0 and an
SD near 1.0.

\input{tables/judge_dynamic_range.tex}

Second, those near-zero scores are a property of the judge, not the text. On
\texttt{qwen3:4b}'s own outputs in the shared overlap sample, three independent
judges --- including two \emph{other small open-weight} models --- all assign real
manipulation and real spread (\texttt{gemma3:4b} mean 2.5, SD 1.0;
\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6} 1.68, 1.22; \texttt{llama3.2:3b} 1.38, 0.60; $n=16$
qwen-subject overlap items), versus \texttt{qwen3:4b}-on-\texttt{qwen3:4b} at
0.13, 0.33. So ``the outputs are simply clean'' is ruled out: a different 4B
open-weight judge sees manipulation in the same text that \texttt{qwen3:4b} scores
at zero.

We are therefore precise about the claim. This is not proof of causal
self-preference; the dominant effect is that \texttt{qwen3:4b} is a badly
calibrated judge that floors almost everything. But the practical hazard is
concrete and it is what the title names: the model you used to grade itself has
near-zero dynamic range exactly where an independent judge finds the largest
signal --- in its own outputs --- and no amount of same-family cross-checking
recovers it. Full diagnostics are regenerated by the committed
\texttt{pressure\_evals.judge\_diagnostics} module.

\subsection{The full picture: same text, opposite conclusions}
\label{sec:res_twojudge}

Pooled across all three subject models, the two judges answer the substantive
question oppositely. Under the open-weight judge (Table~\ref{tab:contrasts_qwen}),
no contrast approaches significance (all permutation $p \geq 0.63$); manipulation
is floor-compressed (mean 0.04--0.11 of 0--8; median 0 in every condition) and
autonomy is at ceiling (mean 3.96--4.00 of 0--4) (Figures~\ref{fig:manip},
\ref{fig:auto}). Under the frontier judge (Table~\ref{tab:contrasts_claude}), the
autonomy instruction significantly reduces manipulation and raises
autonomy-respecting language, both added to pressure and alone, with moderate
effect sizes ($|d|\approx 0.5$--$0.6$); its manipulation means are far from the
floor (baseline 1.87, pressure 2.20, autonomy 1.18, pressure\_autonomy 1.40 out
of 8), roughly 10--40$\times$ the open-weight judge's. Applying Holm--Bonferroni
across the six-test confirmatory family, the four autonomy-instruction contrasts
survive (adjusted $p \leq 0.0052$); the two pressure $-$ baseline contrasts do not
(adjusted $p = 0.28$).

\subsection{The two judges agree at chance}
\label{sec:res_disagree}

The disagreement is not a calibration offset that a linear rescaling would fix.
Across all 180 items and six dimensions (Table~\ref{tab:xjudge}), every weighted
Cohen's $\kappa$ is at or near zero, and composite correlation is weak
(manipulation Pearson $r=0.165$, MAE $=1.58$ of 8). On
\texttt{emotional\_pressure} the open-weight judge returns a constant 0 for every
one of 180 items while the frontier judge averages 0.73 --- the two are on
effectively different scales. Chance-level agreement is the mechanism by which the
same evidence supports opposite conclusions.

\subsection{It is not simply ``the frontier judge scores everything high''}
\label{sec:res_notartifact}

Two checks argue against the obvious innocent explanations. First, the
deterministic keyword audit (Table~\ref{tab:det_audit},
Appendix~\ref{app:additional}) found overt lexical manipulation rare across all
180 outputs (urgency language 1.1\%, emotional-pressure language 0.0\%,
authority/social-proof 1.7\%, tradeoff disclosed 99.4\%). Both judges are
therefore right that the outputs are not \emph{lexically} manipulative; the
frontier judge's non-zero scores reflect graded, contextual coding (e.g. an
omitted cheaper alternative, a buried decline option --- see
Appendix~\ref{app:qualitative}) that a keyword audit and the open-weight judge
miss, not overt tactics. Second, the frontier judge is not uniformly high: it
agrees closely with the open-weight judge exactly where scores sit near the
floor/ceiling (e.g. \texttt{respect\_for\_refusal} means 1.77 vs 1.99), and
diverges on the discriminating dimensions. And the open-weight null is not a
pipeline bug: for matched baseline/pressure outputs of one scenario/model, the
open-weight judge produces distinct, response-specific evidence for each, both
scoring 0 because both preserve disclosure --- ruling out a constant-output
shortcut. An exploratory scenario-clustered OLS on the open-weight scores likewise
found nothing ($R^2=0.026$, $n=180$).

\subsection{Evaluator dependence, and one analysis that looks like a rescue}
\label{sec:res_evaldep}

Averaging the two judges' scores does not wash the effect out: the consensus
autonomy-instruction contrasts stay significant, roughly halved (autonomy $-$
baseline manipulation $-0.367$, $p=0.0010$; pressure\_autonomy $-$ pressure
$-0.389$, $p=0.0021$), while pressure $-$ baseline stays null. So this is a
genuine split, not one outlier judge against a robust null. One robustness
analysis \emph{appears} to restore the null --- excluding the 48 of 180 items
flagged as high-disagreement and recomputing on consensus scores yields all
contrasts non-significant --- but we caution explicitly against reading it as
confirmation. Its exclusion criteria (cross-judge score range, primary/secondary
divergence, top-5\% consensus manipulation) are partly \emph{defined} by the
qwen--claude divergence being tested, so they mechanically remove the items that
carry the frontier judge's signal. The honest reading is ``the effect is
concentrated in the disagreement-flagged subset,'' not ``it disappears under a
neutral filter''; separating those requires a disagreement definition that does
not reference the tested outcome, which we do not have.

\subsection{Adding more open-weight judges does not catch it}
\label{sec:res_secondary}

The two secondary open-weight judges (\texttt{gemma3:4b}, \texttt{llama3.2:3b}),
on the 60-item overlap sample, do not rescue the open-weight judge into agreement
with the frontier judge. Agreement among the three open-weight models is high on
dimensions near the floor/ceiling (\texttt{respect\_for\_refusal} exact 90\%,
\texttt{artificial\_urgency} 86\%) and low on the discriminating ones
(\texttt{deceptive\_omission} 8\%, \texttt{authority\_social\_pressure} 17\%)
(Figure~\ref{fig:judge_agreement}; full tables in Appendix~\ref{app:additional}).
\texttt{llama3.2:3b} left evidence text empty on 89 of 360 scored cells (vs 2 of
360 for \texttt{gemma3:4b}). The three small judges disagree with each \emph{other}
on exactly the dimensions where they disagree with the frontier judge --- so an
open-weight ensemble shares the blind spot rather than covering it.
