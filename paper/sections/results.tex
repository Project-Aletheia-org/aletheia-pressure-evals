\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section reports results in the order they were obtained. Sections
\ref{sec:primary-contrasts}--\ref{sec:multi-judge-agreement-local} are the
original open-weight-judge analysis: a clean null, with no evidence of a
pipeline or scoring-shortcut artifact. Section~\ref{sec:evaluator-sensitivity}
reports the external-judge validation extension, which overturns that null
and is the paper's central finding.

\subsection{Primary confirmatory contrasts: no detectable effect}
\label{sec:primary-contrasts}

both composites, pooled across the three subject models
($n=45$ scenario-model pairs per contrast). All permutation $p$-values are
$\geq 0.63$; all bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals include (or lie
essentially at) zero.

\subsection{Verification that the null result is not a pipeline artifact}
\label{sec:verification-not-artifact}

We manually inspected matched baseline/pressure outputs for the same
scenario and model (\texttt{s01\_notifications}, \texttt{qwen3:4b}): the
judge produces distinct, response-specific evidence text for each, and
both happen to score 0 manipulation because both preserve disclosure and
decline language. This rules out a trivial scoring shortcut (e.g. all
outputs scored identically regardless of content).

\subsection{Independent corroboration via deterministic audit}
\label{sec:deterministic-corroboration}

The code-only text audit across all 180 outputs found urgency language in
1.1\% of responses, emotional-pressure language in 0.0\%, and
authority/social-proof language in 1.7\%, with 99.4\% disclosing the
relevant tradeoff and 91.7\% mentioning the known alternative -- consistent
with the judge's near-zero manipulation scores via a completely different
(non-model, keyword-based) measurement.

\subsection{Exploratory regression}
\label{sec:exploratory-regression}

The scenario-clustered OLS regression
(manipulation\_score $\sim$ pressure + autonomy + pressure$\times$autonomy
+ model) found no coefficient distinguishable from zero (all $p > 0.14$)
and near-zero explanatory power ($R^2 = 0.026$, $n=180$). This is
exploratory and is reported for completeness, not as a secondary
confirmatory finding.

\subsection{Robustness}
\label{sec:robustness}

Excluding the 20 priority-escalated (genuinely disputed) items and
recomputing pressure$-$baseline on manipulation\_score (160/180 remaining,
34 scenario-model pairs): mean diff 0.029, bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.000,
0.088]$, permutation $p=1.000$ -- the null result is unchanged. Further
robustness checks (per-scenario exclusion, per-model exclusion,
bounded-sensitivity analysis for disputed items) were not run as separate
artifacts given the near-uniform null pattern already observed across
scenarios and models; this is an honest limitation of the robustness
analysis, not a claim that these checks were performed.

\subsection{Multi-judge agreement}
\label{sec:multi-judge-agreement-local}

Across the 60-item tri-judge overlap sample, agreement is highest on
\texttt{respect\_for\_refusal} (exact agreement 90\%, mean absolute
disagreement 0.089) and \texttt{artificial\_urgency} (exact agreement
87\%), and lower on \texttt{deceptive\_omission} (exact agreement 8\%) and
\texttt{authority\_social\_pressure} (exact agreement 13\%), consistent
with the floor-compressed score distribution making small integer
disagreements (0 vs.\ 1) more common than substantive disagreement about
whether a response is manipulative overall. \texttt{llama3.2:3b} produced
empty evidence text on 89 of 360 (60 items $\times$ 6 dimensions) scored
cells, versus 2 of 360 for \texttt{gemma3:4b} -- a genuine judge-quality
finding for the smallest of the three models, not a pipeline defect, and
the dominant (though rarely solely decisive) driver of the 89 escalation
flags (Table~\ref{tab:judge_agreement}, full detail in
\texttt{reports/\allowbreak judge\_agreement.csv} and
\texttt{reports/\allowbreak escalation\_reason\_summary.md}).

\subsection{External-judge validation reveals evaluator sensitivity}
\label{sec:evaluator-sensitivity}

Sections 3.1--3.6 establish that the null result under \texttt{qwen3:4b}
is not a pipeline bug, a scoring shortcut, or an artifact of any single
open-weight judge -- it is corroborated by a code-only audit and is broadly
consistent across the two open-weight secondary judges on the dimensions
where those judges agree with each other at all. The question this section
answers is different: does the null survive being scored by a judge from
outside the model family being studied?

\subsubsection{Qwen-only, recomputed, confirms the original null}
\label{sec:qwen-recompute}

As a preliminary check, the Qwen primary-judge analysis was recomputed
from the raw evaluation records independently of the archived results
file. It matches the original on every mean, median, and permutation
$p$-value; bootstrap CI bounds differ by a small amount (the original
analysis script was not preserved verbatim, so the exact resampling
sequence cannot be reproduced bit-for-bit), and both sets of intervals are
tiny and straddle zero. The recompute changes no conclusion.

\label{sec:claude-only}

(Section~\ref{sec:external-judge}), blind to the primary judge's
scores and to everything the primary judge was blind to. Table~\ref{tab:claude-contrasts}
reports the same three preregistered contrasts on Claude's scores alone.
Two of three are significant, with moderate effect sizes.

Descriptively, Claude's manipulation-score means (out of 8) are far from
floored -- baseline 1.87, pressure 2.20, autonomy 1.18,
pressure\_autonomy 1.40, with real within-cell variance (SD
$\approx$1.0--1.16) -- roughly 10--40$\times$ higher than Qwen's in every

Both significant contrasts concern the autonomy-preserving instruction:
adding it on top of pressure, and adding it in place of no instruction,
each significantly reduce manipulation-coded language and increase
autonomy-respecting behavior. The effect is broad rather than narrow: 4--5
of the 6 rubric dimensions are independently significant in the same
direction in both contrasts (full per-dimension table in

\subsubsection{The two judges agree at barely above chance}
\label{sec:qwen-claude-agreement}

Table~\ref{tab:qwen-claude-agreement} reports per-dimension agreement
between the two judges on the identical 180 items. Every Cohen's $\kappa$

operating on effectively different scales. At the composite level,
manipulation\_score agreement is Pearson $r=0.165$, Spearman
$\rho=0.104$, MAE $=1.58$ (of 8); autonomy\_score agreement is Pearson

\subsubsection{Consensus scoring: attenuated but still significant}
\label{sec:consensus-scoring}

Averaging the two judges' scores per item roughly halves the effect size
relative to Claude alone, but the same two contrasts remain significant:
\texttt{pressure\_autonomy}~$-$~\texttt{pressure} on manipulation (mean
diff $-0.389$, 95\% CI $[-0.600,-0.167]$, $p=0.0021$) and on autonomy (mean
diff $+0.278$, CI $[0.122,0.433]$, $p=0.0009$); \texttt{autonomy}~$-$~%
\texttt{baseline} on manipulation (mean diff $-0.367$, CI
$[-0.600,-0.144]$, $p=0.0010$) and on autonomy (mean diff $+0.267$, CI
$[0.111,0.422]$, $p=0.0003$). Per-dimension, the consensus effect is driven
mainly by \texttt{emotional\_pressure}, \texttt{respect\_for\_refusal}, and
\texttt{transparency} (all $p<0.012$ in both contrasts).

\subsubsection{Excluding disputed items: the effect concentrates, it does not vanish}
\label{sec:sensitivity-exclusion}

An item is flagged high-disagreement if any escalation criterion fires
other than \texttt{unverifiable\_evidence} (excluded because that
criterion's evidence-groundedness regex produces a documented false-positive
rate against Claude's quoting style, not a real evidence problem; see
Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}). 48/180 items (27\%) are flagged this
way. On the remaining 132 items, every contrast is non-significant
(full table in Appendix~\ref{app:additional}). This is not an independent
robustness check: several exclusion criteria are partly defined by the same
manipulation-score divergence being tested, so excluding them mechanically
removes many of the items carrying the signal. E's null is read as
\emph{``the effect is concentrated in the disagreement-flagged subset,''}
not as an evaluator-independent refutation of Sections
\ref{sec:claude-only}--\ref{sec:consensus-scoring}.

\subsubsection{The disagreement concentrates in the judge's own outputs}
\label{sec:self-eval-blindspot}

The Claude-detected effect is not evenly spread across the three subject
models. By-model manipulation-score contrasts (Claude judge) are
significant only for \texttt{qwen3:4b} ($p=0.0008$ and $p=0.0031$ for the
two significant pooled contrasts) and null or non-significant for
\texttt{gemma3:4b} ($p=1.00$, $p=0.668$) and \texttt{llama3.2:3b}
($p=0.115$, $p=0.125$, same direction but underpowered at $n=15$).
\texttt{qwen3:4b} is simultaneously a subject model in this experiment and
the primary local judge. Scoring its own outputs, \texttt{qwen3:4b}
assigns a flat, zero-variance manipulation score across all four
conditions for 14 of 15 scenarios (the 15th scores a flat 1 in every
condition). Claude, judging the identical text blind to its authorship,
assigns \texttt{qwen3:4b}'s outputs a clear condition-dependent swing
(pressure 2.87 $\to$ pressure\_autonomy 1.07 $\to$ autonomy 0.73, out of
8). The effect appears in 13--14 of 15 scenarios in the same direction, so
this is a subject-model effect, not a one-off scenario artifact.
Appendix~\ref{app:qualitative} works through one paired example
(\texttt{s15\_account\_creation}, pressure vs.\ pressure\_autonomy) in
full: the two responses differ in exactly the way Claude's scores say they
do -- the pressure-condition response omits the guest-checkout alternative
and gives no easy decline path, the pressure\_autonomy response states
both explicitly -- while Qwen scores both responses identically (0/0),
confirming the divergence tracks real textual content rather than judge
noise on either side.

\subsubsection{Classification: evaluator-sensitive}
\label{sec:classification}

The substantive conclusion -- does the autonomy-preserving instruction
reduce manipulation and increase autonomy-respecting behavior? -- flips
from ``no measurable effect'' (Qwen alone) to ``yes, a significant effect
with moderate effect size'' (Claude alone, and attenuated-but-significant
under consensus scoring), depending entirely on which judge is used, and
the mechanism is traceable to a self-evaluation blind spot rather than
scenario- or single-dimension-specific noise. This study is classified as
\textbf{evaluator-sensitive}: neither judge's verdict is reported as the
answer, and the disagreement itself is reported as the result.
